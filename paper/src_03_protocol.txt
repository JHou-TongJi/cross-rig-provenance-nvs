H1|5. Experimental Protocol
H2|5.1. Data
P|The principal development sequence, 2026-05-22-10-25-21, contains 80 synchronized key frames spanning about 39 seconds, seven RGB cameras and one concatenated LiDAR stream, with per-frame calibration supplying the extrinsics, intrinsics and image dimensions and a separate file the distortion coefficients.
P|Frame 40 serves the geometry acceptance audit, frame 73 the learned-branch shifted-pose evaluation, and frames 24 to 43 the temporal series. The data are challenge reference data under a use agreement and are not redistributed; Section 8 and the Data Availability Statement describe lineage and access.
P|The supplied calibration omits several quantities a fully specified cross-rig transform needs: the LiDAR origin height, the absolute camera mounting positions in a body frame, the body-to-LiDAR extrinsic and the source vehicle dimensions. Where needed the pipeline uses documented team estimates, marked as assumptions in the configuration files.
P|The LiDAR stream that anchors every geometry decision also bounds what the observed class can contain. Figure 4 renders the concatenated sweep top-down under both rig definitions and Figure 5 summarizes its geometry. The target-frame cloud holds 313,196 returns with a median planar range of 11.45 m; 86.5% lie inside 30 m and 93.5% inside 50 m, the 95th percentile reaches 54.57 m and the farthest return 250.13 m. Half the geometric evidence supporting any synthesized view therefore comes from within about 11 m.

P|Two azimuthal sectors carry no return inside 20 m: one of 11 degrees at 110 to 121 degrees from the forward axis, one of 5 degrees at -113 to -108 degrees. Their near-mirror symmetry about the longitudinal axis is what self-occlusion by the ego platform would produce, not a property of the scene. These are the dark wedges in Figure 4, and any target-rig ray falling inside them is unobservable at close range whatever the pose — a floor on unknown coverage for any method relying on this sweep for geometric support; LiDAR silence does not rule out RGB or temporal evidence, and we do not test whether those could recover the sectors.
P|The top-down comparison also quantifies rig overlap. Of the cells occupied under either rig, 68.0% are occupied under both, with 16.0% exclusive to each — consistent with a rigid transform, which relocates structure without creating or destroying it; an asymmetry would have indicated an error in the transform. Panel (c) of Figure 4 was verified to be an absolute difference rather than assumed: it correlates at 0.85 with the absolute difference of the inputs against 0.65 with their mean.
H2|5.2. Two Target Rig Configurations
P|Two target configurations are kept distinct throughout; Table 5 records the parameters that distinguish them; complete intrinsics, distortion coefficients and mount transforms live in the configuration files it names. Rig R1 is the open seven-view reference configuration on a 3.70 × 1.40 × 2.00 m low-speed logistics preset and expresses the intended cross-platform scenario; rig R2 applies a controlled upward translation of about 0.20 m while retaining the source camera arrangement, isolating a pure vertical displacement.
INCLUDE|tables/t4_rigs.txt
P|Neither rig is a measured target-vehicle calibration. Both are configuration files: R1 a reference configuration on an open vehicle preset, R2 an audit configuration isolating one degree of freedom. Obtaining a real calibration would mean replacing the configuration and regenerating results, and would invalidate any mixing of old and new numbers, so every report records its rig identifier and Section 6 reports per rig rather than pooled.
P|The consequence for interpretation is worth stating before any number is read. **No result in this paper characterizes a real target vehicle.** The rigs are instruments for exercising the pipeline and protocol under controlled, known displacements, so Section 7.6's statements about fleet practice are hypotheses to test rather than validated platform findings.
H2|5.3. Evaluation Runs
P|Three families of runs are reported. **Geometry acceptance:** the geometry branch at frame 40, seven cameras, both rigs, giving Table 8 and a separate hole-free check. **Temporal series:** the geometry branch on frames 24 to 43 for one camera under R1, no temporal smoothing, giving Table 10. **Shifted-pose stress test:** the learned A-ONLY route at frame 73 for 21 cases, seven cameras under each of three bands of 5-10, 10-20 and 20-50 cm, perturbations accumulated per Equation (6). The closest source camera is in the input, so this family is Task B.
P|The released inference branch uses frozen structure and A-ONLY checkpoints; no training happens during evaluation. Two resolutions must not be conflated: the shifted-pose audit under R2 is rasterized at 1920 × 1080, while R1 declares a 960 × 540 delivery resolution whose geometry audits are computed on the source-native raster and so are unaffected. The audit uses a 32-layer inverse-depth sweep on a single RTX 3090 with 24 GB.

P|Two denominators are easy to misread. GateFill is a fraction of the hole, not the image: a 44.90% gate fill does not mean 44.90% of the image was recovered. GeneratedFraction is a fraction of the image, bounding the generative module's total influence. LockedChange is an absolute pixel count whose target is exactly zero. We deliberately prescribe no universal accept or reject thresholds for the profile: the protocol reports evidence, and what counts as an acceptable profile depends on the rig, the scene and the downstream use.
P|One property bounds every conclusion drawn from the profile: **no component of it measures correctness.** Observed coverage counts pixels with traceable source support, not pixels whose colour is right. Gate fill counts candidates surviving a cycle test computed on the pipeline's own estimated geometry, so it measures internal consistency, not agreement with the world. The locked-pixel count checks a constraint the implementation enforces structurally. The profile is a traceability and self-consistency instrument; correctness needs an external reference, which protocols P1 to P3 of Table 7 would supply and this evidence base does not contain.
P|We also report the vehicle-risk fraction inside holes, computed against the pipeline's own vehicle-region mask, which monitors whether the completion module is being asked to invent traffic participants; a team reusing this diagnostic needs an equivalent mask of its own, and the final visual coverage and hole-free pass rate, which describe the delivered display image rather than the geometry.
P|Table 6 lists the metric set with its measurement status, which is as much part of the result as the values. Geometry error, semantic preservation and temporal anomaly rate are unmeasured with no proxy substituted; target-view PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS are not applicable rather than unmeasured, because here they would be undefined or compare against the wrong image (Section 7.4).

H2|5.5. What Is Deliberately Not Reported
P|Four absences are intentional and each is a real limitation. No target-view photometric score is reported, for the reason above. No component-wise ablation is reported: the package contains no matched-seed runs against a common target, and estimating gains from the available artefacts would be fabrication. No baseline comparison is reported, because none has been run on this data under this protocol. And no geometric-accuracy figure is reported, because the depth field is LiDAR-constrained with no subset held out.
P|All four are closable without new collection. Table 7 specifies the runs: P5 the component ablation, P6 the same-protocol baseline, P3 the LiDAR holdout for the missing geometric accuracy, and P1 and P2 a setting in which a photometric score is defined rather than improvised. Executing P1 to P6 is the necessary condition for any claim beyond the protocol contribution itself.

H2|6.3. Shifted-Pose Evaluation
P|Table 11 reports the aggregate learned-branch results over the 21 shifted-pose cases and Figure 17 the trend with per-camera spread. Observed coverage falls monotonically with the perturbation band, from 99.32% to 98.59% to 96.79%, overall 98.24%; gate filling falls from 54.86% to 41.49% to 38.34%, and the generated fraction rises from 0.37% to 0.82% to 2.10%, overall 44.90% and 1.10%. Every aggregate in Tables 11 to 13 is an unweighted mean over per-camera ratios, and the choice is consequential for the two metrics whose denominator is the hole rather than the frame. Observed coverage and generated fraction are frame-denominated, and all 21 cases share the same 1920 x 1080 raster, so for those the unweighted and pooled values coincide. Gate fill and vehicle risk are hole-denominated and hole counts differ case by case, so for those they do not: pooled gate fill is 45.39%, 41.57% and 34.48% by band and 37.77% overall, against the 54.86%, 41.49%, 38.34% and 44.90% reported here. The two answer different questions -- the unweighted mean asks how the gate behaves on a typical camera, the pooled rate how it behaves over all hole pixels -- and every gate-fill and vehicle-risk figure in this article is the unweighted mean.

P|This closure is the arithmetic statement of the contract: no fourth category, no unaccounted remainder. Between the narrowest and widest bands the observed share contracts by 2.53 points, 0.80 absorbed by the gate and 1.73 falling through to generation, so gate recovery relative to generation falls from 0.83 to 0.53.
P|In all 21 cases the number of locked observed pixels whose value changed is exactly zero, and Figure 32 maps where the polish did act. The guarantee is structural rather than statistical — the observed region is removed from the modification mask of Equation (20) by construction, so a non-zero count would signal an implementation defect — and we report it as a regression test to be re-checked on every future configuration. The count is taken on the rectified internal raster, which is where Section 3.4 defines the lock. Export redistorts that raster, and resampling interpolates: a delivered pixel near a class boundary can mix observed and recovered contributions, so byte equality is not claimed for the redistorted image and is not measured there. The same resampling raises the question of what label a delivered pixel carries. The contract answers with a conservative rule: a delivered pixel receiving any contribution from a generated pixel is itself generated, and one mixing observed and gate contributions is gate, so a label can only move toward lower evidence. Categorical labels are remapped by nearest neighbour while RGB is interpolated, which is why the rule has to be conservative: the two resamplings need not agree at a class boundary. What we do not report is how many pixels that disagreement touches. Figure 33 shows the two rasters involved. Measuring it is protocol P8 of Table 7, and it is not a byte comparison between the two rasters -- they are different domains and would differ almost everywhere -- but a comparison of the delivered image against the locked reference carried through the same remap, counting cross-class contamination at boundaries.

P|One further check separates two questions the compliance numbers above run together: whether the pipeline behaved, and whether the rules could be broken without anyone noticing. A reference implementation of the specification alone -- Equation (7), Equation (20), the Section 4.11 transitions and the export rule, written from this article and sharing no code with the pipeline -- satisfies six invariants over 20,000 randomized frames, and breaking any one rule makes the matching invariant fail on more than 91% of them, so the invariants are falsifiable rather than vacuous. This says the contract is internally coherent and that violations are detectable in principle. It says nothing about whether GCR-NVS implements it, which is a different claim needing the pipeline and is protocol P5 of Table 7.
