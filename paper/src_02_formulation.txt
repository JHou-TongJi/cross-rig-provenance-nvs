PNI|Throughout, j indexes a camera and a superscript t marks a target-side quantity, so K_j_^t^, D_j_^t^ and T_j_^t^ denote the general target camera j of Table 5 and the task-specific forms above are instantiations of it. P|Task C is the operational goal, Task A the most credible quantifiable proxy, Task B a geometric stress test. **The experiments reported here are Task B plus a geometric audit of a Task C configuration; they are not Task A.** The numbers in Section 6 would mislead if read as leave-one-out results, and the same-name camera in the input is the principal reason the reconstructions resemble the source images.

H2|3.4. The Provenance Contract and Its Cost
P|Each output pixel is assigned to exactly one of three evidence classes:
EQ|7
P|**Observed** pixels are sampled from a calibrated source image through a geometrically valid correspondence and are then locked: no downstream module may alter their value. The label asserts source-image ancestry under a correspondence that is itself estimated, not that the target surface was measured: the depth carrying the correspondence is a dense candidate, so a pixel can be observed in this sense and still land on the wrong surface. Only the LiDAR-anchor subtype below is a direct range measurement. We keep the name because the exported masks and the released code use it, and read *source-supported* wherever the stronger reading would matter. The lock is defined on the rectified internal raster, where every geometric export and the locked-pixel count of Section 6.5 are computed. The redistortion of Section 4.12 resamples that raster into the target camera's distortion domain, so exported display pixels are interpolated by construction: the guarantee is that no module changed an observed value beforehand. **Gate** pixels are geometric holes filled only after a source-target-source cycle and the visibility checks of Section 4.10 gave positive supporting evidence. **Generated** pixels occupy the residual holes, are visual-only and are excluded from every metric and geometric export. The masks are written to disk alongside the RGB, and the system never treats generated RGB as depth or pose evidence.
P|Two further states are not labels of Equation (7). A *LiDAR anchor* is a subtype of observed, restored byte-exactly after the display polish. *Unknown* is the complement of the partition; the delivered RGB is hole-free by construction, so it is empty in the image and survives only as an exported mask. Only the three labels sum to the evidence budget of Table 13.
P|The contract is a permission matrix over modules, not a convention. Table 3 records which module may write which channel; two rules carry the weight. No learned module may write into the observed mask, and no module whose output is generated may contribute to the depth export, the SE(3) optimization or any reported geometric metric.

P|The contract costs something real: the delivered geometry declines to reconstruct regions a vendor optimizing for demonstration quality would fill, and those regions grow with baseline. What it buys is that every quantitative statement has a defined support. H2|4.1. Pipeline Overview
P|Figure 3 shows the end-to-end architecture. Geometric stages complete before any visual-only stage, so a geometric result stays reproducible when the optional visual models are unavailable.

P|One implementation finding is invisible to unit tests and expensive to diagnose. The per-frame calibration stores the seven extrinsics in a non-lexicographic order: back-left, front-left, front-right, back-right, back, front-wide, front-narrow. Consuming it in name order pairs each image with a neighbouring camera's extrinsic and yields reprojections that are self-consistent but physically wrong, with a half-frame void, mirrored parallax and anomalously low coverage.
H2|4.3. LiDAR Anchoring and Visibility Rasterization
P|LiDAR points are transformed into each rectified source camera and rasterized with a z-buffer, fixing the metric scale, establishing occlusion ordering and constituting the hard evidence that constrains learned structure; anchor pixels survive every later visual stage byte-exactly. LiDAR is deliberately not used to paint colour: a concatenated sweep is far too sparse for sky, distant facades and many object surfaces, and rendering points as RGB gives an image whose apparent density reflects point density rather than scene content.
H2|4.4. The Geometry-Only Branch and Source Precedence
P|The L4-SE branch composes the target image from the geometric sources of Table 14 in strict priority order, each writing only where every higher-priority source has declined: LiDAR anchors; the current rectified RGB-D surface; temporally adjacent frames of the same camera, after an SE(3) warp and the consistency gates; adjacent calibrated cameras, under rigid-rig reprojection and visibility tests; and a sparse LiDAR-anchored TSDF. These five are the only classes that may write depth. Everything below them in Table 14 is visual-only and excluded from every geometric export, which is why the branch runs without any learned component and why its coverage in Tables 8 and 10 is re-derivable from calibration and point cloud alone.

H2|4.7. Semantic and Detail Encoding with Anchor Attention
P|Frozen DINOv2 features [16] give semantic context and are stable under the illumination changes that break photometric matching; the released backbone is a frozen ViT-L/14 of hidden dimension 1024, with ImageNet-normalized inputs at 364 × 644, while a ViT-B/14 of dimension 768 conditions the structure network and patch tokens from several intermediate layers. Freezing is deliberate: the training corpus is small relative to the encoder's capacity.
P|Because a patch-token backbone discards the high-frequency structure that matters for lane markings, poles and barriers, a lightweight convolutional detail branch of NAFNet-style blocks [42] runs in parallel at higher resolution and feature upsampling [17] restores boundary sharpness before fusion.
P|For each selected LiDAR anchor X_n_, features are sampled from every source camera in which it is visible and aggregated with a four-head attention block [45], so the aggregation learns which views to trust rather than averaging them. The rendered anchor feature map supplements dense geometry and may not alter anchor depth.

H2|4.10. The Source-Target-Source Cycle Gate
P|Geometric holes are target regions with no valid warped source support. Some are recoverable, the surface having been observed while the correspondence failed on a depth-ordering ambiguity, a boundary effect or a marginal validity test; the rest are genuinely unobserved. The gate separates them by requiring a closed cycle: a candidate is projected into a source observation and back, and accepted only if the position, depth and colour residuals fall within bounds, the depth ordering agrees with the target z-buffer, and the region is not flagged dynamic.
P|These values are fixed in the released inference configuration. Depth ordering accepts a candidate whose camera-space depth exceeds the z-buffer by at most max(0.10 m, 0.02 z), so thin distant structure is not culled by depth noise. The dynamic-region test is a Mask R-CNN ResNet-50-FPN segmenter [48] at score threshold 0.5 over person, bicycle, car, motorcycle, bus and truck, dilated by 5 pixels. Fusion uses the temperatures of Equation (19), 0.05 photometric and 0.50 baseline, and drops views below a 0.1 cosine-similarity floor. Equation (18) normalizes over the valid views only, so an invalid view removes a contributor without darkening the pixel, and the plane-sweep path re-masks and renormalizes after smoothing; where no view is valid the pixel passes to the gate as a hole. Failed candidates remain unknown, which is why gate fill falls as the perturbation grows.

H2|4.12. Export, Reproducibility and Cost
P|For every camera and frame the pipeline exports RGB, depth, validity, provenance, hole and unknown masks, a machine-readable report and a lineage record covering source sequence and frame, target rig, resolution, code revision, checkpoint hash, seed, device, elapsed time and the metric scope under which the numbers may be quoted — the field that stops a geometry-branch figure being quoted later as a learned-branch result. Render times of roughly 8 to 31 seconds per camera make this an offline conversion workload, not a real-time renderer.
