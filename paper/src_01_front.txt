ABSTRACT|Reusing a recorded perception corpus on a re-specified sensor rig is attractive for low-speed L4 logistics programmes, but the target platform is usually never driven, so no target-view image exists to score a reprojection against. This article specifies and audits machinery for that regime rather than validating a reconstruction system. A provenance contract partitions every delivered pixel into observed, gate-recovered or generated, locks observed pixels on the rectified internal raster, and forbids visual-only completion from writing depth or pose evidence, with a module permission matrix that makes the partition enforceable in compositing code rather than documented afterwards. An evidence profile - traceable coverage, gate acceptance under an explicit cycle test, generated fraction, locked-pixel compliance, per-camera risk and temporal spread - replaces a photometric score that is undefined without a reference. We audit both on GCR-NVS, a geometry-first seven-camera RGB-LiDAR pipeline: the three classes account for the delivered image exactly and no locked pixel is modified in any of 21 cases. Those are consistency audits, not measurements of fidelity. The demonstration is a proxy - one sequence, parameterized rather than measured target rigs, reprojections retaining the same-name source camera - so no cross-rig synthesis, geometric-accuracy or downstream claim is made.
KEYWORDS|cross-platform camera-data reuse; cross-rig view reprojection; RGB-LiDAR fusion; multi-camera geometry; data provenance; autonomous logistics vehicles; SE(3) rig transformation; visibility reasoning; sensor reconfiguration; auditable evidence profile
H1|1. Introduction
P|Electric and autonomous vehicle programmes accumulate large volumes of synchronized camera and LiDAR data. Reusing that corpus on a new platform would avoid repeating road collection, recalibration and annotation each time the sensor suite is revised. We take as the premise of this work that low-speed L4 logistics platforms are produced in small volumes and re-specified often enough that a fresh collection campaign per revision is uneconomic. That premise motivates the problem; it is not measured here, and no result in this paper depends on it.
P|The reuse problem is not a conventional image-translation task. A target vehicle may place its cameras at a different height and baseline, use different focal lengths and distortion, and see surfaces occluded in the source rig, so resizing, cropping or unconstrained generation cannot guarantee a target pixel corresponds to a physically valid scene point. A model trained on silently hallucinated road surface learns a road that was never there.
P|Neural view synthesis has advanced rapidly, from radiance fields [1-4] to explicit Gaussian representations [5] and feed-forward reconstruction from sparse input [6-8], with diffusion priors where the input is too sparse to constrain the scene [9,10]. Driving reuse demands a property standard image metrics do not express: measured evidence must remain distinguishable from inferred appearance. A hallucinated pixel may look plausible while violating road topology, where a conservative renderer would expose the unknown region instead.
H2|1.1. The No-Ground-Truth Regime
P|A second difficulty dominates practice. Conventional evaluation needs synchronized imagery from the target vehicle at the same place and time as the source; such a double-rig collection is expensive, must be jointly calibrated, and usually does not exist while the algorithm is being developed.
P|A common but unsound response improvises a reference from the same-named source camera and reports PSNR or SSIM against it; Section 7.4 shows why that inverts the intended ranking. The correct response is an evidence profile: how much of the target image a calibrated observation supports, how much was recovered under an explicit consistency test, how much was generated, and whether any observed pixel changed.
H2|1.2. Approach
P|The pipeline pairs a seven-camera RGB-LiDAR source rig with a parameterized seven-view target rig. Two branches, named L4-SE and A-ONLY after the internal components they wrap and carrying no expansion beyond that, share the same calibration, rig transform and provenance contract but report separately: L4-SE supplies calibration, rectification, SE(3) rig motion, LiDAR anchoring and audit artefacts with no learned component; GCR-NVS adds dense learned structure, semantic correspondence and controlled residual completion on the same geometry. Visual polishing never modifies metric depth, LiDAR anchors or rigid-pose optimization.
P|The design principle can be stated in one sentence: **the pipeline is permitted to make a target image look complete, but it is never permitted to make it look observed.** Everything else in the architecture follows from enforcing that separation at each stage.
H2|1.3. Contributions
P|Five contributions follow; the first two are the ones we ask to be judged.
NUM|**A provenance contract.** A per-pixel partition into observed, gate-recovered and generated, with a module permission matrix fixing which stage may write which channel. Observed pixels are locked byte-exactly on the rectified internal raster the lock is defined over; export redistorts by interpolation, so byte equality is claimed before that resampling and not after. No visual-only module may write depth, pose or any reported geometric quantity, and the contract is enforced in the compositing code rather than documented alongside it.
NUM|**An evaluation protocol for the no-ground-truth regime.** An evidence profile -- observed coverage, gate acceptance under an explicit cycle test, generated fraction, locked-pixel compliance, per-camera risk and temporal spread -- in which every quantity has a defined support and a stated measurement status, replacing a photometric score that is undefined without a reference. Section 5.4 says where the profile is gameable: with the same-name source camera in the input, observed coverage is copy-sensitive and is not a capability measure.
NUM|**A task taxonomy** separating strict leave-one-camera-out reconstruction, same-name small-baseline reprojection and true cross-vehicle synthesis, and identifying which one each result addresses.
NUM|**A reference implementation**, geometry-first: metric LiDAR anchors, dense monocular depth candidates, self-supervised semantic correspondence, a rigid SE(3) rig transform and gated residual synthesis under the provenance mask, including the fusion logit that explains why small-baseline results resemble their source images.
NUM|**A proxy audit.** One sequence, two parameterized rigs, 21 shifted-pose cases and 20 consecutive frames. The three classes account for the delivered image exactly with zero modification of locked pixels. Two findings outlast this pipeline: a seven-camera mean conceals a 4.9-fold concentration of vehicle-region risk in one camera, and a delivered video can be checked against its declared construction from the pixels alone.
P|What this does not establish: the shifted-pose cases are same-name reprojections, not leave-one-camera-out synthesis; both rigs are parameterized configurations, so no result characterizes a real platform; and we report no geometric error against a depth holdout, no ablation and no downstream retention. Table 7 specifies the eight runs that would close these, seven needing no new collection.
H1|2. Related Work
H2|2.1. View Synthesis, Depth and Visual Features
P|Neural Radiance Fields render new views by ray integration [1], with extensions to unbounded scenes [3], improved reconstruction [2] and hash-grid primitives [4]; Gaussian Splatting substitutes explicit primitives for real-time rendering [5], and plane-based representations [11,12] trade generality for a cheaper model. All are evaluated by scoring a held-out image from the same capture, which measures interpolation inside the observed view manifold.
P|At corpus scale per-scene optimization is impractical, so generalizable methods condition on image features [2,6], feed-forward Gaussian methods predict primitives in one pass [7,8], and diffusion approaches treat synthesis as conditional generation [9,10,13]. These are powerful because they hallucinate plausibly, which is why their output is not measured evidence unless the pipeline records which pixels it produced. Ours admits such a prior only behind a mask and marks its output generated.
P|Dense monocular depth has matured from transformer baselines [14] to large-scale mixed supervision giving robust metric depth [15], but these are a shape prior, not metric ground truth: scale drift, thin-structure smoothing and boundary bleeding are documented failure modes invisible in a depth colourmap. Self-supervised DINOv2 features [16] transfer well to correspondence where photometric consistency is weak, and feature upsampling recovers boundaries patch-token backbones discard [17].
H2|2.2. Calibration, Rigid Rigs and Driving-Data Reuse
P|We use the Brown-Conrady model [18] with supplied coefficients, compute all geometry in a rectified pinhole domain and remap only for display [19,20], and represent motion in SE(3) [21], within the metric-consistency problem the SLAM literature addresses [22]. A physically meaningful target rig applies one vehicle transform to fixed mounts, whereas independent per-camera warps give images that are individually plausible and jointly impossible (Section 3.3).
P|Reusing driving data is established: public datasets [23-25] made multi-sensor corpora available, simulators [26] offered configurability at the cost of a domain gap, and sensor-realistic resimulation reconstructs and re-renders the scene [27-35]. Our problem differs in that the target configuration is a supplied file rather than a property of the collection, and the output feeds perception, which raises an unlabelled hallucination from an aesthetic defect to a data-integrity one.
H2|2.3. Downstream Consumers, Provenance and Positioning
P|The consumer is usually a perception stack, and modern camera-based stacks lift features into a shared bird's-eye-view space [36,37] and increasingly fuse the whole driving task [38]. Such stacks are sensitive to extrinsics, so their performance on reprojected input follows geometric fidelity rather than perceptual quality, which makes a downstream retention experiment the most informative validation available without target-view ground truth. We do not run one; it is P4 in Table 7.
P|Separating what a model measured from what it inferred recurs in trustworthy machine learning [39], and image restoration supplies our machinery for residual regions [40-42]. Documentation practice records provenance at corpus and checkpoint level [43,44]; carrying it to the pixel and exporting it is rare, since common practice computes a validity mask internally and discards it. We treat the provenance map as a first-class output alongside RGB and depth (Section 3.4, Table 3): the same kind of accountability record datasheets and model cards established, but per pixel and enforced in code rather than written afterwards.
P|This work differs from all three lines on four axes at once. Evaluation assumes no target-view reference exists, rather than holding one out from the same capture. Provenance is exported per pixel rather than discarded. Observed pixels are locked, so no later visual module can overwrite evidence. And the target viewpoint is one rigid vehicle transform applied to fixed mounts, not an independent warp per camera. Each has precedent alone; we are not aware of a driving-reuse system enforcing all four together, and it is the combination Section 5 audits.
