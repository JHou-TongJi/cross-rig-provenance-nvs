H1|7. Discussion
H2|7.1. Large-Baseline Disocclusion Is an Observability Boundary, Not a Model Deficiency
P|When a target view exposes a surface absent from every source image and temporal neighbour, no geometric method can recover its true texture. This is a statement about the information content of the capture, not the capacity of the model, and the correct output is an unknown mask; any pixel written into it is a hypothesis however it was produced.
P|The generated fraction grows from 0.37% to 2.10% across the bands. It tracks this boundary without measuring it, since it also moves with depth error, gate thresholds, source selection and completion policy: even at 20-50 cm an average of 96.79% of the frame stays supported by a calibrated observation and 2.10% is hypothesis. That ratio is favourable precisely because the displacement is small; extrapolating it to a genuine platform change would be unjustified, since the relationship is scene-dependent and must be measured per configuration.
H2|7.2. Focal Length, Camera Geometry and the Concentration of Risk
P|The front narrow camera is the worst performer on geometry coverage under both rigs and carries the highest vehicle-risk fraction, and two measured optical asymmetries accompany that deficit. Induced image-space displacement scales with focal length, and this camera has 3.8 times the focal length of the front wide, which shares its raster, and 3.3 to 3.4 times the surrounds once each focal length is scaled to a common image width (Table 1); its field of view is 2.8 times narrower than theirs, so the same rig motion sweeps it across more of its field of view while its narrow field of view reduces the overlap neighbours could supply.
P|The second asymmetry is on the input side, and Section 6.4 gives its numbers. A rotating LiDAR of fixed angular resolution places returns by the solid angle a field subtends rather than by pixel count, so a narrow field at high pixel count collects fewer returns per pixel: the telephoto receives 2,053 per megapixel of its native raster against 10,175 to 25,596 for the rest of the rig, and 8,214 against 18,435 for the next sparsest once every camera is counted on the common evaluation raster. The direction is raster-independent and the magnitude is not, and per solid angle the telephoto is the densest camera on the rig rather than the sparsest, so this is a statement about pixels per return and not about the scanner neglecting that direction. So it needs the most interpolation per return and receives the least support per pixel, which is a rig-design consideration rather than a model one and is invisible to any aggregate metric.
P|Both asymmetries are measured; neither is shown to cause the deficit. One sequence cannot separate optics from the scene content this camera happens to face, and settling it needs a controlled sweep over focal length, output resolution and pose on a fixed scene. We report the association and the mechanism that would explain it, and stop there.
P|The consequence is direct: a seven-camera mean of 4.94% is comfortable, the 24.23% carried by one camera is not, and a deployment decision taken on the mean is taken on the wrong quantity. Per-camera reporting is mandatory rather than supplementary.
P|The remedy is not a stronger completion model. When a quarter of a camera's holes fall on vehicle regions the correct response is instance-aware handling: detect and track the instances, then recover them from a temporally adjacent observation or leave them explicitly unknown. Generating a plausible vehicle is worse than leaving the hole, because a fabricated traffic participant propagates silently into whatever consumes the corpus.
P|The back-right camera shows a related but distinct pattern, with the largest inter-rig coverage difference at 6.06 percentage points; its behaviour is dominated by rig geometry rather than focal length, since it sits where R1's forward and pitch components combine least favourably with the existing mounting angle.
H2|7.3. Dynamics and Temporal Interpolation
P|The pipeline assumes a static scene between the observations it fuses; moving vehicles, pedestrians, vegetation and the finite duration of a LiDAR sweep all violate that. Consistency gates, source priority and dynamic-region masks mitigate it, but instance-level scene flow is not estimated, so long-horizon fusion can ghost on movers. The demonstration video interpolates between real anchors 0.500 s apart, 80 of 4,741 displayed frames being evidence and 98.3% interpolation (Section 6.2); a reader judging temporal quality from it would be reading the interpolator.
H2|7.4. Why Conventional Image Metrics Are Insufficient Here
P|In the no-target-ground-truth setting PSNR, SSIM [49] and LPIPS [50] are either undefined or actively misleading, and it is worth being precise about which.
P|They are undefined without a target-view reference, the situation for every result in Section 6, and misleading when one is improvised from the same-named source camera: by Equation (19) that camera dominates the fusion weight at small baseline, so a system that copies it scores well and one that correctly renders the induced parallax scores worse. The metric would reward the behaviour the system exists to avoid.
P|What we report instead is an evidence profile: coverage with a traceable source, gate acceptance under an explicit cycle test, bounded generated fraction, exact locked-pixel compliance and per-camera risk. It is weaker than a photometric score in not measuring how correct the delivered colour is, and stronger in that every quantity has a defined support and a stated measurement status. Once a double-rig collection exists the conventional metrics become defined and should be added alongside the profile, not substituted for it.
H2|7.5. The Same-Name Camera Caveat
P|The 21 shifted-pose cases are Task B: the same-name source camera is in the input, a translation-only perturbation leaves its optical axis unchanged, and Equation (19) gives it a dominant weight. They demonstrate correct pose handling, a consistent extrinsic chain, continuous motion, a discriminating gate and an enforced contract. They do not demonstrate synthesis of a viewpoint the rig cannot see, so the 98.24% observed coverage must never be quoted as evidence of that capability. P1 of Table 7 needs no new hardware and admits an exact photometric ground truth.
H2|7.6. Implications for L4 Logistics Vehicle Programmes
P|Three suggestions follow for a fleet operator, offered as hypotheses rather than conclusions: they rest on one sequence, two parameterized rigs and a single learned-branch key frame, and the threats of Section 7.7 apply to all three. **First**, the target rig should be a versioned configuration artefact, not a constant in code; the 2.6-point difference between two rigs separated by a modest displacement and a two-degree pitch shows how quickly results become incomparable, so a metric quoted without a rig identifier is unusable. This follows from the paired audit of Table 8 and is the best supported of the three. **Second**, acceptance criteria should be written in terms of evidence rather than appearance: a hole-free requirement is trivially met by a generative model, whereas exported provenance masks, zero locked-pixel modification and stated bounds on coverage and generated fraction are checkable and not satisfiable by hallucination. **Third**, per-camera thresholds likely need setting from each camera's geometry, since a telephoto and a wide surround did not degrade alike under the same motion.
P|Three parts of this work are portable to a team with its own multi-camera corpus, independently of our pipeline: the provenance contract of Section 3.4 and Table 3, which specifies what a reprojection tool must export and what each stage may write; the evidence profile of Section 5.4, which needs only masks such a tool already computes; and the per-camera risk diagnostic of Section 6.4.
H2|7.7. Threats to Validity
BUL|**Single sequence, single condition.** All reported numbers come from one sequence, in one location, under one set of lighting and weather conditions, on one road type. Coverage is a function of scene geometry and appearance: an urban canyon, a tunnel, an open highway, night, rain or low sun would each produce different values, and low-texture or specular conditions would additionally stress the plane sweep and the cycle gate in ways this sequence cannot reveal. The reported figures characterize the pipeline on this data, not the pipeline in general, and no variance estimate across conditions is available.
BUL|**Single frame for the learned branch.** The 21 shifted-pose cases are 21 camera-pose combinations at one key frame, not 21 independent scenes. They share scene content, and the per-camera variation reported in Section 6.4 partly reflects what each camera happens to be looking at in that frame.
BUL|**Estimated rig parameters.** The LiDAR ground height, body-frame camera positions and vehicle dimensions used to construct the target rigs are documented team estimates, not measured values. They affect the absolute interpretation of the target geometry, though not the internal consistency of the comparison between the two rigs.
BUL|**One point of sensitivity, not a curve.** The estimate above raises the obvious question of how much the reported coverage would move if a rig parameter were wrong, and the paired audit answers it at exactly one point. The two rigs differ in more than one respect, and the difference must be read accordingly. R1 sits 0.10 m forward, 0.35 m up and 2 degrees pitched from the source while R2 is approximately 0.20 m up, so their separation combines a forward translation, about 0.15 m of height, a rotation, and a change of delivery raster from 960 x 540 to 1920 x 1080. Mean geometry coverage differs by 2.6 points between them. That is a measured response to a known *configuration* change, not to a controlled vertical displacement, and no part of it can be attributed to height alone. The response is also strongly camera-dependent -- the back-right camera moves 6.06 points and the front wide 0.6 -- so no single ratio extrapolates to other magnitudes or to rotation. Bounding the estimates properly would need a sweep over rig parameters, which is a configuration change and compute rather than new collection, and which we have not run.
BUL|**Proxy metrics throughout.** Every quantity in Section 6 is a coverage, provenance or consistency proxy. None is a fidelity measurement against an independent reference, and no combination of them establishes that the delivered colour is correct.
BUL|**No independent geometric validation.** The depth field is constrained by LiDAR and evaluated on data that includes the constraining points. A held-out LiDAR subset is required before any statement about geometric accuracy can be made, and none is made here.
H1|8. Reproducibility, Data Lineage and Ethical Data Use
H2|8.1. Artefact Organization and Data Lineage
P|The implementation has two branches with separate manifests: a geometry branch that runs without any learned component, and an inference-only learned branch consuming the same calibration and rig configuration. A run records the source sequence and frame, target rig, resolution, seed, code revision, checkpoint hash, device, elapsed time and output paths. Optional visual models can be disabled without altering the geometric exports.
P|The inference configuration is fully specified, and we give the identifiers rather than pointing at a run report. The frozen project checkpoints are t0_structure_rectified_v2_dinob14_320x180_c48_best.pt for the structure-constraint network, adapter_step_001000.pt for the A-ONLY residual adapter and anyup_multi_backbone.pth for the feature upsampler; the frozen third-party models are DA3Metric-Large for dense metric depth, dinov2_vitl14 for semantic features at a 364 x 644 input, and dinov2_vitb14 as the structure network's own feature backbone. Section 4.5 gives the resulting parameter counts, obtained by instantiating the released modules rather than quoted from a specification.
P|Training is a different matter. The released package is inference-only: model definitions, frozen weights and the configuration that consumes them, but not the training scripts, losses, optimizer settings, schedules or data splits that produced those weights. No training happens during evaluation, so the evaluation path is deterministic given its inputs; but the release carries neither the road-collection data nor the checkpoints, and we cannot show that the evaluation frames were excluded from the training or checkpoint selection of the frozen weights, because the splits that produced them are not ours to publish. Reproduction from this release alone is therefore not possible, and we do not claim it. We state the split explicitly, because a reproducibility claim that does not say which half it covers is worth little. The geometry branch is reproducible in principle from the released code given the sequence and the calibration: it carries no learned component, and Tables 8, 9 and 10 come from it. **The learned branch is a non-reproducible demonstration.** Its checkpoints, training data, losses, optimizer settings and selection splits are not ours to release, so Tables 11 to 13 and every A-ONLY figure should be read as a record of what one frozen configuration did, not as a result another group could regenerate and check. Retraining rather than rerunning would need material this artefact does not carry, and we prefer to say so than to imply otherwise.
P|Table 14 records the eleven lineage classes and, for each, the provenance label of Equation (7) it produces, whether it may export depth, and whether it is eligible for SE(3) optimization. The table is machine-readable in the artefact package and is the authority consulted at composition time rather than a post-hoc description of it. Its priority column also defines compositing order: a lower-priority class may write only where every higher-priority class has declined.

H2|8.2. Ethical and Compliance Position
P|The reference data are used under a challenge data-use agreement for development, self-evaluation, demonstration and review, and are not redistributed. Raw RGB, point clouds, calibration and sensitive derived data are excluded from any public repository, and licence plates, faces and sensitive geographic information are anonymized to the provider's requirements before release.
P|Two commitments are verifiable from the exported artefacts rather than resting on assertion: results are produced by batch execution of the released code, not manual per-frame substitution, and generated pixels are separated from measured geometry by the provenance and hole masks. The target vehicle parameters are a team-defined reference preset, not a manufacturer measurement; if a measured calibration becomes available the configuration must be versioned upward, results regenerated, and old and new not pooled.
H1|9. Limitations and Future Work
H2|9.1. Limitations
PBL|Section 7.7 states the four threats that bound every number in this paper: a single sequence and condition, a single key frame for the learned branch, estimated rather than measured rig parameters, and proxy metrics with no independent geometric validation. They are not repeated here. One further limitation is a property of the model rather than the evidence.
BUL|**Modelling gaps.** Dynamic-object scene flow is not modelled at instance level, higher-resolution coarse-to-fine depth is not implemented, and the predicted uncertainty is not calibrated against any external reference, so it orders pixels by confidence without carrying a probabilistic guarantee.
H2|9.2. Required Experiments and Metrics
P|Table 7 specifies eight runs that would convert the present proxy evidence into a defensible benchmark. Seven need no new collection — only the existing sequence, the frozen checkpoints and compute. P1 is the highest-value item: hiding one camera and reconstructing it from the remaining six yields an exact photometric ground truth, directly testing the capability this protocol cannot establish. P7, the synchronized double rig, is the only route to a true Task C benchmark and the only one that cannot be started today.
P|The reporting set should extend along four axes: geometric accuracy as depth MAE and RMSE against held-out LiDAR, split into near, mid and far; semantic preservation as feature cosine similarity or mIoU; temporal consistency as optical-flow warping error rather than coverage variance; and downstream retention on an unmodified perception stack, which most directly answers fitness for purpose. Generated regions should stay labelled even when a stronger model improves their appearance: the provenance channel matters most exactly when the eye cannot find the completion.
H1|10. Conclusions
P|We specified a provenance contract and a no-ground-truth evaluation protocol for cross-rig camera-data reuse, with a geometry-first pipeline implementing them on a calibrated seven-camera RGB-LiDAR platform. The design makes observability explicit: LiDAR and calibrated source RGB establish metric evidence, a shared SE(3) rig transform defines the target geometry, cycle and visibility gates reject unsupported correspondences, and residual completion is isolated behind a mask labelled generated and excluded from every geometric export.
P|The results characterize that protocol rather than certify the pipeline. The geometry branch reconstructs all seven target cameras under two parameterized rigs at 94.07% and 96.66% mean coverage, passes the display-completeness audit with no near-black component above 3 pixels, and holds coverage to a 0.44-point standard deviation across 20 consecutive frames of one camera under one rig. The learned branch, on 21 same-name small-baseline cases that are Task B rather than cross-rig synthesis, attains 98.24% mean observed coverage, recovers 44.90% of holes through the gate as a per-camera mean, 37.77% pooled over all hole pixels, confines generated pixels to 1.10% of the image, and modifies zero locked pixels in every case.
P|The boundaries of that evidence are set out in Sections 1.3 and 9.1 and softened nowhere. The operational one bears repeating: because we report no held-out geometric error and no downstream retention, **this paper does not demonstrate that the reprojected corpus is fit for training or validating a perception stack.** It contributes an auditable route to that determination.
P|Two findings are worth testing beyond this system: degradation under rig displacement is strongly camera-dependent, so a seven-camera mean can conceal a fivefold concentration of vehicle-region risk; and an acceptance specification written in terms of evidence is checkable, whereas a hole-free requirement is satisfiable by hallucination. Until a measured double-rig collection exists, the appropriate posture is the one adopted here: reconstruct what was observed, recover only what can be demonstrated, generate the remainder under an explicit label, and never let the third category be mistaken for the first.
